What does Validator Clock show?

Validator Clock follows validator-set rounds and election windows. Choose TON, Everscale or Tycho Testnet to see the network's clock, validator lists, recorded stakes and available history.

When do validator elections start and end?

The election window is calculated from the active round's end timestamp and the network's election timing settings. It opens a configured number of seconds before the round ends and closes at a second configured offset. Use the selected network's displayed dates rather than assuming that all networks share one schedule.

The clock labels three phases:

  • Before elections: the election window for the active round has not opened.
  • Elections open: the current time falls inside that window.
  • After elections: that window has closed; the active round may still be running.

The upcoming-window display advances after elections close. An election window ending does not mean the active validator round ends at the same moment. Timing is derived from network configuration, so an operator should also verify the network's current rules and their own submission status.

How do I find the next TON validator election?

Open the TON validator dashboard. Check the network name, data freshness and election phase, then read the election window and countdown. The recorded section provides absolute UTC dates for the window associated with its recorded round. The interactive clock shows the upcoming window in your local timezone.

What do the blue and green rounds mean?

The dashboard uses blue and green to distinguish alternating validator rounds and their related history. The colour is a visual grouping, not a rating of validator quality or reliability. Use the round ID and start/end dates when comparing records or sharing a result.

How many validators are active?

The recorded network snapshot reports the size of the active validator set. Expand its validator table to inspect the public keys, available stakes and consensus weights. A validator count does not necessarily equal a count of independent operators or physical machines.

How should I read rewards and APR?

Rewards describe recorded round data. APR annualizes those observations using the dashboard's round-cycle calculation. It can be unavailable when stake or rewards are missing, and it is not a promise of future returns. The methodology page explains the formula and the average displayed in the history panels.

Why does a snapshot say it is stale?

The server may be waiting for its next successful collection, or the recorded round may already have ended. Read the observation timestamp before using its values. The interactive clock updates separately, while the recorded HTML snapshot remains an explicitly dated reference for that page load.

Is Tycho Testnet the same as mainnet?

No. The Tycho page monitors the configured test network. Its validator participation, timings and token figures should not be presented as Tycho mainnet measurements.
